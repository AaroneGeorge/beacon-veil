### Project Summary

One requirement of this RFP cannot be met in full on today's LEZ, and we say so first. RFP-022 Functionality 7 asks the vault to release on a trustless proof of a LEZ burn, and the trust model says no signer is trusted. On LEZ v0.4.0-rc3 a block header has no state root (`lez/common/src/block.rs:51-57`, read 2026-11-12) and public execution is not proven. So the fact that the LEZ block holding a burn is valid and canonical cannot be proven to Ethereum. We prove everything else that can be proven, isolate that one fact behind an interface, and offer a staged release. Stage 1 is in this grant. Its residual trust is the canonical Bedrock header, attested optimistically, with a named arbiter on dispute. We word the outbound milestone as "stage-1 anchor agreed at M0" and never as a trustless release.

The inbound leg has no such gap. The bridge consumes the attestation from our RFP-021 proposal, which runs the Helios consensus code from `a16z/helios` 0.12.0 unmodified (TODO-VERIFY: link the audit report that covers it). A deposit commits to its LEZ destination inside a hash, so nothing on Ethereum names it. The claim is one private LEZ transaction, and it is zero-knowledge also when the destination is public.

This proposal and our RFP-021 proposal are one project, filed as two issues. The combined price is $88,000 for about 20 calendar weeks. The RFP-022 share is $56,000. The audit is a separate allowance that Logos contracts and pays, and it is not in the $88,000.

What runs today. A prototype is public at https://github.com/TODO-OWNER/beacon-veil (TODO-OWNER: URL). Its README starts with clean-clone commands. It holds a Foundry vault with tests (fixed denominations, native ETH and ERC-20, a USDT-style token, caps, freeze, a release path behind the RISC Zero Groth16 verifier), the bridge program, the burn proof guest and a CLI. The light-client side has a measured result: unmodified Helios applied five real mainnet updates in the RISC Zero executor at 74.4 million to 89.3 million cycles each (`evidence/v1/v1-exec.json`). #### 2. Privacy construction (F2, F8, F13, Priv1 to Priv7)

What stays public is the amount, the token and the timing. We make them uninformative about which counterparty they pair with.

- Amounts. Fixed denominations per token. Every deposit of one (token, denomination) pair sits in one anonymity set. Committed amounts are Soft Requirement 1. The commitment layout leaves room for them, and the migration path is written in `docs/bridge/OUTBOUND.md`.
- Commitment. `commitment = sha256("wb/c/v1" || secret || destBinding || token || denom)`. The secret derives from the LEZ wallet seed on a fixed path. The destination binding is `sha256("wb/dest/v1" || bridge_instance || dest_account)`. Nothing on Ethereum names the destination (F2, Priv3).
- Nullifiers. A deposit has a nullifier `sha256("wb/n/v1" || secret)`. The LEZ nullifier account is `PDA(bridge, sha256("bridge/null/v1" || bridge_instance || deposit_nullifier))`.
- Fee payer. A claim is a privacy-preserving transaction paid from the claimer's private account, so no public funding source shows. The release fee goes to whoever calls `release`, and the recipient and the fee are bound inside the burn note, so a relayer cannot redirect or overcharge (Security 3). Fees are a fixed amount per denomination, so no user-specific value appears (F13).
- Residual correlations, listed. A claim against root R shows that the deposit came no later than R. The CLI and the mini-app tell the user to wait and use the latest root. The header record a claim reads is visible. An execution RPC provider that serves a proof sees what the claimant asks about. Network-level correlation is out of scope and is disclosed (Priv6).
- Anonymity sets on a testnet. A testnet has no real population. The privacy suite simulates populations and reports set sizes per token and denomination, and says plainly that the real set is small.

#### 3. Inbound: vault, deposit tree, private claim (F1 to F4, F17, R1, R3)

- Vault (Solidity, OpenZeppelin v5.7.0). `deposit(token, denomIndex, commitment)` takes native ETH (`msg.value` equals the denomination, no WETH step) or an ERC-20 through `SafeERC20`, so tokens that return nothing from `transfer` work. The received balance must equal the denomination, so fee-on-transfer tokens are refused. The commitment becomes a leaf in an incremental SHA-256 tree (the Ethereum deposit contract's algorithm, CC0). The root is stored at a fixed slot that does not depend on the compiler's layout.
- Step 1, public and shared. Anyone attests "the vault's root slot holds root R at a finalised header" through RFP-021. The statement lands in a root receipt that every claimant against R shares. It names no deposit and no destination.
- Step 2, private. The user's device proves that some leaf under R opens to a commitment bound to the destination. The transaction checks the receipt, creates a fresh nullifier, and credits the wrapped balance. It reveals the nullifier, the token and the denomination. A failed step aborts the whole transaction, so the deposit stays claimable (R1).
- Destinations (F4, U6). Default: the claimer's own private account. Option: a public destination, with a separate private claimer. The claim is still zero-knowledge, because the opening is proven privately. Only the credit is visible.
- Uniqueness (F3, R3). The statement about the root is shared by every deposit under it, so it cannot be the only key. The key is the per-deposit nullifier scoped to the bridge instance (`docs/DECISIONS.md` D-33).
- Rule for clients. A claim must run as a privacy-preserving transaction, because the program cannot see its own placement and a public claim would publish the secret. The CLI sends claims only that way.

#### 4. Outbound: what can be proven, and the stages (F5 to F7, Security 2, 3, 8)

| Statement the vault needs | Provable on LEZ v0.4.0-rc3? |
|---|---|
| A burn with a hidden recipient is in the bridge's burn accumulator | Yes. A RISC Zero guest proves membership and a Groth16 wrap is verified on Ethereum. Built in the spike |
| The accumulator root follows from the contents of a chain of LEZ blocks | Yes, by rebuilding it from block bytes in a guest. Design only in the prototype |
| That block is inscribed in a Bedrock block k deep | Yes. Design only |
| The LEZ blocks are valid and the Bedrock chain is the canonical one | **No.** The header has no state root and public execution is not proven. Bedrock finality time is unreconciled: the roadmap says 18 hours and the node settings suggest about 10 minutes (TODO-REFRESH: ask Logos) |

LEZ's own bridge to its base layer releases on a sequencer quorum signature. A bridge to Ethereum cannot be more trustless than the zone it leaves. We state that as the baseline.

| Stage | What makes the vault accept a root | Prototype | Residual trust |
|---|---|---|---|

| 2 | Root rebuilt from LEZ block bytes, inscribed in a Bedrock block k deep | Design only | None |

| 4 | Optimistic Bedrock checkpoint: bonded poster, challenge window, slashable equivocation. The window is also the release delay | **Stub only:** `StubLezAnchor`, owner-posted, fixed delay | One honest watcher in the window |
| 5 | Disputes go to a named arbiter set | Design only | **The arbiter set. This is the stated residual trust of stage 1** |
| later | A zkVM dispute proof replaces the arbiter. Then direct verification once LEZ headers commit to state | Proposal text | |

In the prototype the anchor is a stub. Whoever holds its owner key can make any root final and release any registered asset up to the window cap. Nothing in the prototype claims trustless release. The M4 deliverable is the stage-1 anchor agreed with Logos at M0. Two platform changes would remove the gap: LEZ headers that commit to state, and Bedrock headers or checkpoints that commit to the note roots.

The burn: the holder burns one denomination from a private or public account. The program builds the accumulator leaf from the token and denomination it really debited, so the proof cannot overstate them. The note binds the recipient and the fee. The guest reveals recipient, amount, token, fee, nullifier, vault and chain id. `Vault.release(seal, journal)` checks the journal length and version, the pairing, the token and denomination, an unspent nullifier, the anchor, the Groth16 proof and the window cap, then pays the recipient and the relayer. A failed payout reverts and leaves the entitlement intact (R2).

#### 5. Recovery from the wallet seed (F9, R5, R7)

`bridge_cli recover` needs the mnemonic and chain state only. Inbound: for index 0, 1, 2 and so on it derives the deposit secret and tests every `Deposit` log's commitment against each of the wallet's own accounts as destination. It stops after 20 indexes with no match. For each match it reads the nullifier account on LEZ to report claimed or open. Outbound: it reads the accumulator leaf records, derives each burn secret, opens each memo (a 36-byte value only the owner can decrypt) and reports whether the vault spent the nullifier. No secret is generated at deposit or burn time, so none can be lost. TODO-TEST: restore from an empty client and find every open deposit and burn.

#### 6. Registry, caps, fees, freeze, finality depth (F10 to F14, Security 4, 5)

Prototype vault: an admin role registers tokens with their fixed denominations, deregistration stops deposits but not releases, a freeze role pauses deposits and releases separately, and per-token caps apply over fixed windows. Foundry tests cover each. M4 moves the LEZ side to the RFP-001 and RFP-002 libraries, adds global rolling caps, a fee switch with a denomination choice (wrapped token, native LEZ or a mix), and a per-bridge finality depth.

Finality. A claim needs a header the light client applied as finalised (two epochs, about 13 minutes on mainnet). A bridge that wants a deeper margin requires `record.slot + k <= head.slot`. A change to k does not invalidate a claim that was valid under the old k.

Proposed defaults, to be tuned with Logos at M4 from the anonymity-set simulation. These are proposals and not measurements: a release delay equal to the stage-1 challenge window, and a per-window cap of 10 percent of the vault's balance of that token. TODO-OWNER: confirm or change.

The freeze authority works at token and protocol granularity only. It cannot freeze one user's holdings, because the protocol does not know whose they are. That is a designed consequence of the privacy guarantees and is documented as such.

#### 7. Pairing and multi-entity configurations (F15, F16)

A bridge instance is `PDA(bridge, sha256("bridge/instance/v1" || borsh(config)))`. The config names one chain id, one vault, the attest program and the attest instance. Every message carries the config and the program re-hashes it, so it cannot change. Every account the bridge writes is keyed by the instance, so a second entity with its own config gets disjoint accounts and its own wrapped balances. The vault binds once to one LEZ program and instance and checks both on release. Foundry tests: `test_instanceBindsOnce`, `test_releaseNeedsBoundInstance`, `test_releaseRefusesWrongPairing`. One program pair serves one chain pair, as the RFP assumes. TODO-TEST: two instances on one sequencer cannot touch each other's accounts.

#### 8. Failsafe position and migration (Security 8, 9)

Migration. The vault has no upgrade path. Its verifier image id and anchor are fixed at deployment (`test_releaseRefusesWrongImage`). A change of proof system or anchor is a new vault and a new bridge instance. Old vaults stay open for releases and are never closed, so in-flight burns and claims stay valid indefinitely (R4). Users move by burning on the old instance and depositing on the new one, or by redeeming. The migration steps are in `docs/bridge/TRUST.md` section 6.

Failsafe. Working position, to be agreed with Logos at M0: no failsafe beyond the freeze authority, rolling caps and the release delay. A refund of unclaimed deposits would need proof that they were not claimed on LEZ, which is the proof path whose failure triggers the failsafe. An admin-driven recovery would identify who owns what, which the protocol cannot know and the RFP forbids. The RFP allows a reasoned "no". TODO-OWNER: decide. The bridge track's draft in `docs/bridge/TRUST.md` section 6 proposes a depositor-proves-opening recovery for unclaimed deposits. It must first show how the vault learns that a deposit was not minted on LEZ, or a deposit could be paid out twice.

#### 11. Trust model and what each third party learns (Security 7, Priv4, Priv6, R9)

The full text is `docs/bridge/TRUST.md`, and the attestation side is `docs/TRUST.md`.

| Participant | Role | Can it block? | Can it steal, redirect or deanonymise? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Root attester | Posts "vault root = R at header H" for everyone | No: anyone can run it, including the user | No. The statement is checked on chain and names no deposit |
| Claim prover | The user's own device | n/a | n/a |
| Burn prover | Anyone with the witness, normally the user | No: anyone can prove | A prover that gets the witness learns the recipient. It cannot change the recipient or the fee |
